### Narrative

Generally speaking, employment rates have grown since 1969\. However, the rate of growth has not been equal for rural and nonrural areas. The 2022 employment rate for nonrural areas grew nearly two and a half times (2.45) as compared to its 1969 rate, as seen in Figure 2\. On the other hand, for rural areas the employment rate less than doubled (1.65). This difference shows the widening gap between rural and nonrural employment.

To gain more understanding of how employment has changed, we can look at three sectors of employment (local services, tradable goods, and tradable services) and how they have changed over the past two decades in rural and nonrural areas. In Figure 7A, we can see that the share of local services has not changed much for both rural and nonrural areas between 2001 and 2022\.

When looking at the other two sectors, though, we see a change–namely a decrease in tradable goods (e.g. manufacturing, agriculture) and an increase in tradable services (e.g. Software as a Service, consulting, IT services, digital marketing) for both rural and nonrural areas. This is likely due to growth in technology and accompanying services.

Despite these similar trends, when looking at the specific shares for 2022 (Figure 7B) we can see how different the distributions of these two sectors are for rural and nonrural areas. For nonrural areas, tradable services and tradable goods represented similar shares (30% and 31%, respectively). In contrast, tradable services represented a much smaller share for rural areas (17% vs 43% for tradable goods). Local services represented a similar share in both rural and nonrural areas (39%). 

The low proportion of tradable services in rural areas shows an important area for improvement, especially as tradable services continue to represent a larger portion of employment nationwide. One source of financing that can help bridge this gap is venture capital investment, which will help scalable, high-growth startups get off the ground.

While community banks are essential for rural business financing (especially for businesses in the tradable goods), venture capital is key to scaling enterprises, particularly in the tradable services sector where rural areas continue to lag, contributing to employment growth disparities in the knowledge economy. 

### Design

For the improved design of Figure 2, I decided to break each line into their own chart. The reason for this is that each data point is being compared against its own baseline (rural or nonrural), which took me a second to figure out when they were overlapping. Even after I put them side-by-side the gap is still evident. 

At the end of this assignment, a few possible improvements occurred to me (which I didn't have time to design, but sketched below): to relabel the “100%” y-axis tick to be “baseline”, and replace the following tick labels with an arrow up saying “growth in employment”. I think this would simplify the chart for the audience. Generally, I’m not sure if talking about 250% growth is very intuitive to the layperson. Also, 100% may make sense from a mathematical perspective, but it may mislead the reader to think that employment was 100% in 1969\. I would also add a horizontal reference line that represents “doubled” compared to baseline. This would more strongly connect to my narrative that for nonrural areas the growth more than doubled and for rural areas it less than doubled.

For Figure 7, I decided to eliminate the intermediary data points and only compare the first and last time points. The trends followed a roughly consistent pattern between rural and nonrural sectors so I didn’t mind making this simplification, and the intermediary data points didn’t strictly relate to the report. I think this helped show an inverse relationship between tradable goods and services (for both rural and nonrural). By making local services grey, I was able to emphasize tradable services and tradable goods which better aligned with the report. I also changed the colors for those two categories since they were previously using the rural and nonrural colors. I didn’t include data labels because I wanted the difference to be quick and uncluttered; however, I included the specific data points for 2022 in the accompanying bar chart. I didn’t include a legend here because I imagine the bar chart would go right below the slope chart. The bar chart is important to include because it shows that the three sectors represent a whole, which is not immediately clear in the slope chart or original chart. Because the takeaway talks about shares of each sector, hopefully the bar chart helps reinforce that data structure.
